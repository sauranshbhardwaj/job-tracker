# Product

## Register

product

## Users

A 2027 computer science graduate seeking US, Canadian and UK software engineering and related entry-level roles, open to all locations in those countries and work arrangements. The product preserves work-authorization uncertainty and supports searches requiring future employer sponsorship.

## Product Purpose

Save the time spent searching multiple job sources. Publish one fixed daily edition at 6:30 PM Eastern, with up to 100 recently published employer postings that satisfy US, Canada or UK location, zero-experience entry-level, and work-authorization rules. Allow clear entry-level postings with unstated start dates, label uncertainty, preserve application links, and let the user decide whether their skills or interests match. Never use resume matching to hide jobs.

## Brand Personality

Simple, reliable, trustworthy. The user explicitly requested a minimal interface with no fluff and no notifications.

## Anti-references

Decorative dashboards, promotional content, match scores, unnecessary onboarding, automatic applications, and silent filtering based on the resume.

## Design Principles

- Applying is the primary action.
- Separate evidence from assumptions.
- Hold unclear dates and entry-level evidence in a separate review queue. Never pad an edition to reach 100.
- Applied and skipped jobs leave the edition immediately, without replacement. Published membership stays fixed; newly discovered disqualifying evidence can withdraw a job.
- Never relabel discovery or update time as original publication time.
- Make source failures and stale data visible.
- Keep collection working without AI access.
- Require positive US, Canada or UK location evidence in the main views; hold ambiguous geography in audit views.
- Keep application filters separate from source administration and retain personal history when a posting no longer qualifies.

## Accessibility & Inclusion

Implementation defaults: semantic forms and tables, keyboard access, visible focus, readable contrast, text labels for all states, and responsive layouts. No user-specific accommodations were requested.

US roles assume OPT/STEM OPT and future sponsorship. Canada and UK roles assume no existing work authorization and require employer sponsorship. Unknown sponsorship remains clearly labeled. The cap is 100 combined across all countries.
